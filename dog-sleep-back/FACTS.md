# dog-sleep-back: facts and sources

Each claim in the film is listed below, source first. Source types:
- **STUDY**: peer-reviewed research
- **VET ARTICLE**: a consumer article labelled vet-reviewed or vet-verified
- **FRAMING**: the film's own wording, not a factual claim

> **No study on butt direction was found.** Searches found no peer-reviewed research on which way dogs face their owners while sleeping. The "trust" and "pack" explanations come only from consumer pet articles, so the film says **"may"** every time.

> **What I couldn't open (network).** This environment blocks pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, mdpi.com, dogstrust.org.uk, dogster.com, betterpet.com and tryfi.com. The details below come from search-engine extracts of those pages, not from my own reading of the full text. Items marked ⚠ need one check by you, or by me once those hosts are allowed.

## Claim 1: the hook (line 1, frame 1)
"Why does your dog sleep with its butt facing you?"
- **Source:** none needed (it's a question). **FRAMING.**

## Claim 2: the myth, struck out (line 2)
"It's rude, or the dog is gassy."
- **Source:** none. This is the common assumption the film sets up so it can strike it out. **FRAMING.** The film never claims that gas is never a cause.
- Note: the Dogster article says to see a vet if the dog's rear smells bad or it keeps licking or biting at it (possible anal-gland problems). We don't say this on screen. It doesn't contradict the film, because the film only strikes out "it's rude".

## Claim 3: "Vets say it may be the opposite." / "Turning its back to you may signal trust." (lines 3–4)
- **Source:** Dogster, *Why Do Dogs Sleep With Their Bum Facing You? 5 Vet-Verified Reasons for This Behavior*, https://www.dogster.com/lifestyle/why-do-dogs-sleep-with-their-bum-facing-you/ **VET ARTICLE** (labelled "Vet-Verified"). It names comfort, trust and protectiveness as possible reasons.
  - ⚠ The search extract doesn't name the vet reviewer for this article. A reviewer is named on a different Dogster article (Dr. Maja Platisa, DVM MRCVS, on dogs sleeping on their back). Please confirm a named DVM on the page before posting. If there isn't one, change line 3 to "Experts say…" or "Pet behaviour writers say…"
- **Supporting, not vet-checked:** BetterPet (https://betterpet.com/why-do-dogs-sleep-with-their-bum-facing-you) and Fi (https://blog.tryfi.com/why-do-dogs-sleep-with-their-bum-facing-you) give the same trust and vulnerability explanation. **VET ARTICLE?** Unconfirmed: I couldn't check whether either page is vet-reviewed.
- **Wording in film:** "may" in both lines ✔

## Claim 4: the mechanism, wild ancestor card (line 5)
"In packs, dogs may sleep back-to-back so someone's always watching."
- **Source:** Dogster, *Dog sleeping positions*, https://www.dogster.com/lifestyle/dog-sleeping-positions/ **VET ARTICLE** (⚠ reviewer not confirmed). It links back-to-back sleeping to wild canine pack ancestry and shared warmth.
- **Supporting:** Tractive blog on sleeping positions ("may echo ancestral pack behavior") and iHeartDogs *9 Reasons Your Dog Always Sleeps With Their Back Against You*. These are consumer articles, not vet-checked.
- ⚠ **Gap:** the sources mostly say back-to-back sleeping is for **bonding and warmth**. Only some blog sources mention **watching for danger**. "So someone's always watching" goes further than the stronger sources. It's covered by "may", but if you want the safest line: *"In packs, dogs may sleep back-to-back to stay warm and safe."* That would mean re-recording line 5, so it's your call.

## Claim 5: the evidence (line 6, on screen "86%")
"A UK study found over eighty-six percent of dogs near people at night chose to be close."
- **Source:** *Sleep Duration and Behaviours: A Descriptive Analysis of a Cohort of Dogs up to 12 Months of Age.* Animals (MDPI), 2020, 10(7):1172. PMC7401528. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7401528/ · https://www.mdpi.com/2076-2615/10/7/1172 **STUDY** (peer-reviewed). Part of the Dogs Trust *Generation Pup* cohort, UK.
  - Finding: most owners left their dogs overnight in a room without people. **Of dogs that did have access to people during the night, 86.7% (at 16 weeks) and 86.8% (at 12 months) chose to be around people.**
  - Sample: surveys from owners of 2,332 dogs at 16 weeks and 1,091 at 12 months.
  - Limits: owners reported the behaviour (it wasn't observed), and the dogs were puppies up to 12 months old. The study is about **being near people at night, not sleeping position or which way the dog faces.**
- **Press release:** Dogs Trust, World Sleep Day, https://www.dogstrust.org.uk/about-us/media-centre/world-sleep-day. It rounds the figure to 87%.
- **On screen:** "86%+" with the caption "Dogs Trust study, 2020". "Over 86%" is accurate (86.7 and 86.8). ✔
- Wording note: "dogs near people at night" is slightly loose. The study means dogs that *could reach* people at night. The on-screen tag will say "dogs that could reach people at night".
