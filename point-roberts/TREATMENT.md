# Point Roberts: Treatment

Style: Copperplate Engraving (`engraving`). 1080×1920 vertical, 24 fps, 35 s. Narration word for word (Kokoro `am_michael`, calm), script captions on a paper slip. No storyboard stop.

## 1. Three candidate structures
A. **States of one plate (chosen).** One engraved chart of the Pacific Northwest is cut, then re-worked: the coast is engraved, the 49th parallel is ruled across it, the peninsula's tip gets its wash, and a dotted road is cut through Canada and back. The plate "changes state" as history does. It ends on the finished, titled plate.
B. **A book of plates.** Four small plates turned like pages, one fact each. Clear, but page turns break the geography; the viewer loses where the peninsula is.
C. **Proof and copper.** Mirrored copper becomes the true print at "So its tip became American". A strong reveal, but a mirrored map confuses in a 35 s phone video.

A wins: one place, one camera (a loupe over the sheet), and each sentence adds one engraved layer. That matches the map/site-plan grammar in the style: place → inset → route → finished plate.

## 2. Logline and arc
An engraved chart rules a straight line along the 49th parallel and finds it has cut a peninsula's tip off from its own country.
- Setup: the coast engraves itself; a ring is scribed around one tiny tip.
- Turn: the burin rules the 49th parallel; the camera rides it to the peninsula; the tip, alone below the line, takes the United States' wash.
- Ending: a dotted road is cut from the tip up through Canada and back down into Washington, with two crossings numbered. Pull out to the finished plate, title engraved, held.

## 3. Benchmark
19th-century coast-survey and boundary-commission charts. Learn: water-lining (lines following the coast out to sea), a ruled graticule, roman capitals for countries, italics for water, dotted roads, numbered references. Don't take any specific chart's layout, cartouche or lettering.

## 5. Colour
Two values until shot 2. Then one wash family: pale buff ochre for United States land, laid after the line. The tip's ochre bloom at "So its tip became American" is the one colour event. Sea stays paper and line.

## 6. Sound
Workroom tone with a faint clock; burin hiss for every cut (coast, ruled line, dotted road, chatter on the dots), a tick per glyph, a soft brush swish and water plip for the wash. Music: harpsichord, plucked and dry while lines are cut; a solo cello note enters when the wash arrives on the tip; resolves under the finished plate. One real silence: half a beat before "So its tip became American". Voice ~10 dB over music, −14 LUFS.

## 7. Type
Captions in Pinyon Script on a deckled paper slip, bottom centre, above the phone UI. Plate lettering in Bodoni Moda, cut glyph by glyph: CANADA, UNITED STATES, *Strait of Georgia*, *Vancouver*, *Pacific Ocean*, the 49° label, the title. Labels stay clear of the caption slip.
