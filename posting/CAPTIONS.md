### 2. Why dogs tilt their heads — `pet-cam-test/style/test.mp4` · cover `covers/pet-head-tilt.jpg`

**YouTube title**
Why Does Your Dog Tilt Its Head When You Talk? 🐶

**YouTube description**
One idea: your dog's muzzle blocks part of your face, so it tilts to see your mouth. In one owner survey, long-muzzled dogs did tilt more often than flat-faced ones (71% vs 52%).

But a 2021 study found something stranger. Dogs that had learned their toys' names tilted their heads very often when they heard those names. Most other dogs rarely did. And each dog tended to tilt to the same side, every time.

So that little tilt may mean one thing: your dog is listening hard. 👂 Say the name of their favorite ball, and watch.

Sources:
• Stanley Coren, Psychology Today: the muzzle idea and the owner survey (582 owners)
• Sommese et al., Animal Cognition (2021): head tilting in dogs that know their toys' names

### 3. What your dog understands when you talk — `dog-words/dog-words.mp4` · cover `covers/pet-89-words.jpg`

**YouTube title**
How Many Words Does Your Dog Actually Understand?

**YouTube description**
More than you think. In one survey, owners said their dogs responded to 89 words, on average. Their name, "sit" and "come" were the top three.

But a brain scan study found something cooler. Dogs process WHAT you say on one side of the brain, and HOW you say it on the other. Their reward area lit up most when praise words came with a happy tone.

So a flat "good dog" doesn't hit the same. Say it like you mean it. 🧠❤️

Sources:
• Reid, Reeve & Jacques, Applied Animal Behaviour Science (2021), Dalhousie University: 165 dogs
• Andics et al., Science (2016): brain scans of 13 awake dogs

**YouTube title**
What Happens to Your Body When You Quit Vaping 💨

**YouTube description**
A pixel body scan of quitting nicotine:
⏱️ Hour 2: half your nicotine is gone
😤 Days 1–3: withdrawal peaks — cravings, irritable, anxious, foggy
🔑 Why so hard? Your brain grew extra nicotine receptors (nicotine is the key, receptors are the locks)
📉 Weeks 1–4: cravings fade over the first month
✅ Weeks 6–12: the extra receptors are gone — back to normal
😌 Month 3+: less anxiety, less stress

The first 3 days are the hardest. It gets easier. 💪 Send this to someone quitting.

• Jones & Benowitz, ACNP: nicotine's ~2-hour half-life
• National Cancer Institute: handling nicotine withdrawal
• NIDA: how nicotine affects the brain; receptor recovery (Staley et al.)
• Taylor et al., BMJ (2014): mental health after quitting

General information, not medical advice.
