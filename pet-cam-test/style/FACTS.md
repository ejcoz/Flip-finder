# Pet Cam demo — facts ("Why does your dog tilt its head?")

Retrieved 2026-11-19. Every on-screen and spoken claim, with its source.

| In the film | Claim | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| "One idea: its muzzle blocks part of your face." + card *the view* | A hypothesis that the muzzle hides the lower part of a speaker's face, so dogs tilt to see it | Stanley Coren, *Psychology Today*, "Why Some Dogs Tilt Their Heads When You Talk to Them" (2013): https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/canine-corner/201312/why-some-dogs-tilt-their-heads-when-you-talk-to-them | Presented as a theory ("One idea", "THEORY: VIEW BLOCKED?") |
| "In one owner survey, long-muzzled dogs tilted more often than flat-faced ones." + card *the survey*: 582 owners, 71% vs 52% | 582 owners responded; 52% of owners of flat-faced (brachycephalic) dogs reported frequent tilting; 71% for longer-muzzled breeds | Coren, same post (582 respondents, 52% brachycephalic); 71% figure as reported in coverage of the survey, e.g. Seattle Humane: https://www.seattlehumane.org/2014/01/10/the-adorable-head-tilt-favorite-photo-opportunity-or-something-more/ | An informal online owner survey, not a controlled study; the film says "one owner survey" |
| "But a 2021 study found something stranger. Dogs that had learned their toys' names tilted their heads very often when they heard those names. Most other dogs rarely did." + card *the study*: 40 dogs, 7 word learners, 33 typical | Gifted word-learner dogs very often tilted their heads on hearing a named toy; typical dogs rarely did | Sommese, Miklósi, Pogány, Temesi, Dror & Fugazza, "An exploratory analysis of head-tilting in dogs", *Animal Cognition* (2021), doi:10.1007/s10071-021-01571-8; ELTE press release: https://ttkold.elte.hu/en/content/talk-to-tilt-head-tilting-in-dogs.t.5018; Phys.org: https://phys.org/news/2021-10-talk-to-tilt-tilting-dogs.html | Small, exploratory sample; some coverage counts 6 gifted dogs instead of 7 |
| "And each dog tended to tilt to the same side." + "SAME SIDE ×3" | The side of the tilt was consistent for each gifted dog over time | Same study and coverage | — |
| "So that little tilt may mean one thing. Your dog is listening hard." + card *inside the head* | The authors suggest tilting is linked to processing meaningful information | Same study; ELTE release | Hedged ("may mean"); the x-ray is illustrative, not a brain map |

## Deliberately not claimed
- The widely quoted "43%" tilting rate: not found in any source I could check.
- That only clever dogs tilt: the authors caution against that reading.
