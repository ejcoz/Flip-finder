# Paper to the Moon — treatment

Style: Pixel Countdown Science. 1080×1920, 30 fps, 37.5 s, English, no voice. Built sound-first on the sonification model (STYLE.md §7).

## Idea
Fold a sheet of paper in half 42 times and it is thick enough to reach the Moon. The viewer counts the folds. Every fold is a note one semitone up, and the scenery lurches down on each one, so exponential growth is both seen and heard.

## Structure (chosen: count-along with two climaxes)
Other options weighed: a countdown challenge ("fold a paper 8 times right now"), kept as the CTA; and a myth-on-trial ("can you fold paper more than 7 times?"), kept as the record beat.

## Facts and sources
- A sheet of office paper is about 0.1 mm thick. Thickness after n folds = 0.1 mm × 2ⁿ.
- Fold 12 ≈ 41 cm; fold 21 ≈ 210 m; fold 23 ≈ 839 m (Burj Khalifa: 828 m); fold 27 ≈ 13.4 km (Everest: 8,849 m; jets cruise ~11 km); fold 30 ≈ 107 km (Kármán line: 100 km); fold 32 ≈ 429 km (ISS: ~400 km).
- Fold 42 ≈ 439,805 km; the Moon's average distance is 384,400 km.
- Fold 103 ≈ 1.0×10²⁷ m ≈ 107 billion light-years; the observable universe is ≈ 93 billion light-years across.
- Record: Britney Gallivan folded a single sheet 12 times in 2002 (she also derived the folding limit formula). Folding an ordinary sheet past about 7 times by hand is the common practical limit.
- Landmark heights on the log scale: coin 2 mm, mug 10 cm, person 1.7 m, house 6 m, 30 floors ≈ 100 m, Sun 150 million km, nearest star 4.2 ly, Milky Way ≈ 100,000 ly, Andromeda ≈ 2.5 million ly.
