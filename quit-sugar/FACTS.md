# You cut added sugar — facts

Retrieved 2026-11-19. The film's timeline runs to ~3 months because the taste-reset evidence is a 3-month study; no claim is made about 2 weeks.

| On screen | Claim | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| DAY 1 · "UNDER 25G A DAY IS THE WHO IDEAL"; readout to 20 g | WHO: free sugars under 10% of energy (strong recommendation); under 5% (~25 g, ~6 teaspoons) for extra benefit (conditional) | WHO 2015 guideline: https://www.who.int/mediacentre/news/releases/2015/sugar-guideline/en/ | The readout's grams are an illustrative day |
| WEEK 1 · "LESS FUEL FOR TOOTH DECAY"; TEETH callout "LOWER DECAY RISK" | Higher rates of dental caries when free sugars exceed 10% of energy | Same WHO guideline (evidence on dental caries) | "Week 1" marks the habit, not a measured dental change |
| MONTH 3 · "FOOD TASTES ~40% SWEETER"; SWEETNESS chart | After 3 months on a lower-sugar diet, people rated puddings as sweeter (about 40% sweeter than controls rated them) | Wise et al., Monell Center, *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* (online 2015): https://www.newswise.com/articles/low-sugar-diet-makes-foods-taste-sweeter-but-does-not-change-preferred-level-of-sweetness | Small study (13 vs 16); funded by PepsiCo and Monell; preferred sweetness didn't change; chart shape illustrative |
| GO BACK? · "YOUR TASTE BOUNCES BACK FAST" | When participants returned to their own diets, sugar intake and sweetness judgements went back to baseline | Same study | — |
| STICK WITH IT · "THE RESET LASTS WHILE YOU DO" | The effect held while intake stayed low and faded when it rose | Same study | Inference from the rebound result |

## Not claimed
- Weight loss, energy, skin, "sugar addiction" or organ changes (organs don't change in the film; amber particles are illustrative).
