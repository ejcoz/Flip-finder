# 23 People — treatment

Style: Pixel Countdown Science. 1080×1920, 30 fps, 35 s, English, no voice. Built sound-first on the sonification model (STYLE.md §7).

## Why it can go viral
- **A question everyone answers wrong.** "How many people until two share a birthday?" The viewer guesses in the first four seconds, and the answer (23) is far below what most expect.
- **A call to action that creates comments.** "Comment your birthday. Find your twin below." Each viewer has a reason to comment and to read other comments, and with enough comments matches really do appear.
- **Picture and sound are one event.** Every guest is a note; every handshake line is a note in a rising run. The runs get longer as the room fills, so you hear the pairs explode.

## Structure (chosen: count-along with two climaxes)
Other options weighed: a split verdict ("your guess vs the truth"), and a countdown challenge; neither shows why the answer is so small. Counting guests while the web of pairs grows shows the reason.

## Facts and sources
- Birthday problem, 365 equally likely days, ignoring leap years: P(n) = 1 − ∏ₖ₌₀ⁿ⁻¹ (365 − k)/365. n = 23 → 50.7 %; n = 15 → 25.3 %; n = 70 → 99.92 %. (Standard result; e.g. Feller, *An Introduction to Probability Theory*.)
- Pairs among n people = n(n − 1)/2: 15 → 105; 23 → 253.
- "Most say 183" is a soft claim: 183 (half of 365) is the classic intuitive guess reported in teaching literature, not a measured survey. Change to "MANY SAY 183" if you want it stricter.
- The on-screen match (two guests, "MAY 9") is an illustration of a match at 23 guests, not a simulation result: at 23 people a match happens about half the time.
