# Why does your dog yawn when you yawn? — treatment

Pet Cam · 1080×1920 · 30 fps · 39.1 s · loops · narrator Kokoro `af_heart` 0.95 · facts in FACTS.md

## Brief changes from the last post (low retention at the start)
- Frame 1 is the dog **mid-yawn in an extreme close-up** (zoom 2.75 on the mouth), the question already whole on screen in the big hook caption, the `YAWN` tag pinned to the mouth. No build-in, no fade.
- The narrator speaks on frame 1: line 1 starts at 0.00 s (the clip's own lead-in is 0.03 s).
- Scenes: home, the owner-vs-stranger CAM split, the dog's-eye view.

## Three structures
1. **The mystery** (chosen): the behaviour caught in the act → the myth struck → the experiment as an evolving split → one takeover card (heart, then brain) → the payoff → the test at home → loop. The topic is a single experiment with a clean three-way comparison, which a split screen shows better than words.
2. **The experiment re-enacted**: the whole film as the lab: owner and stranger take turns. Rejected: it loses the home and the viewer's own dog, which the ending ("try it tonight") needs.
3. **The test first**: open on "yawn at your dog right now", then explain. Rejected: the brief wants the question on frame 1.

## Logline and arc
Your dog's yawn isn't tiredness or nerves: it catches yours, and mostly yours.
Setup (the yawn + the myth) → turn (the owner > stranger > fake split; the flat heart rate) → ending (it's tuned to you; try it tonight; loop).

## Benchmark
Learn from the pet explainers that perform: behaviour in extreme close-up on frame 1, one pinned tag, the myth struck in red, one evolving takeover card, a progress strip, a loop. Don't learn: their characters, wording or music.

## Cue map
80 BPM (bar = 3.0 s), Fmaj7 – Am7 – Dm9 – Bbmaj7: a sleepy, slightly lazy colour for the topic. Pad + plucked arpeggio from bar 1, soft kick and shaker from bar 2. Ducked −10 dB under the voice. Cuts sit on the lines (a whip 0.15 s before each scene's first line); the key caption box pops on its first word.

## Subtitles and titles
The style's two-tier caption, word-synced to the voice: grey lead-in, key phrase in a selection box coloured by meaning (question amber, myth / fake red, facts blue, the answer green). Line 1 uses the big hook caption and is whole from frame 1. Digits on screen ("25", "40 to 60%"), spelled out in the voice. Burned in; `subtitles_en.srt` for upload.

## Compared with the style demo (head tilt)
Different: the opening (an extreme close-up mid-behaviour vs a living-room wide), the signature shot (a three-way condition split with tallies vs three dogs tilting), the card (heart traces → brain vs x-ray → study icons), the score (80 BPM sleepy progression vs 90 BPM major), the ending (a push back into the opening close-up vs a ball rolling in). Shared: the camera grammar (locked scenes, whips) is the style itself.
