# You stop drinking — facts

Retrieved 2026-11-19. Every on-screen claim, its source and the wording it rests on.

| On screen | Claim | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| DAY 1 · "YOUR LIVER CLEARS ABOUT 1 DRINK/HOUR"; LIVER callout "CLEARING ALCOHOL"; readout falling to 0% | The liver metabolises alcohol at a fixed rate, roughly one standard drink per hour | NIAAA Alcohol Alert No. 35 (1997): "The liver can metabolize only a certain amount of alcohol per hour, regardless of the amount that has been consumed" (https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916062737/http://pubs.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/aa35.htm); "one standard drink … per hour": nutrition textbook (https://openwa.pressbooks.pub/tccnutrition/chapter/chapter-13-alcohol/) | "About"; varies by person; the readout is illustrative, not a BAC value |
| DAYS 2-3 · "SLEEP CAN BE ROUGH AT FIRST"; BRAIN callout "RESETTING SLEEP" | Alcohol suppresses REM sleep; sleep can be disturbed when drinking stops (REM rebound, withdrawal) | Review of ~20 lab studies reported by Scientific American (https://www.scientificamerican.com/podcast/episode/nightcap-drink-disrupts-important-s-13-01-29/); Drinkaware on withdrawal (https://www.drinkaware.co.uk/facts/health-effects-of-alcohol/general-health-effects/alcohol-withdrawal-symptoms) | Hedged ("can be") |
| HEAVY DRINKER? · "TALK TO A DOCTOR BEFORE YOU STOP" | For people dependent on alcohol, stopping suddenly can be dangerous, even life-threatening; get medical help first | NHS: "Get medical help before you stop or reduce drinking if you get withdrawal symptoms" (https://www.nhs.uk/content-widgets/health-a-to-z/alcohol-use-disorder); Drinkaware: "stopping suddenly can be dangerous and even life-threatening" | Safety beat, deliberately in every version |
| WEEKS 1-2 · "7 IN 10 SAY THEY SLEPT BETTER"; SLEEP chart | 71% (2018 cohort) / 72% (2019 cohort) of Dry January participants reported sleeping better | University of Sussex (Dr Richard de Visser): https://www.sussex.ac.uk/broadcast/read/50562 and 2019 evaluation (https://www.drugsandalcohol.ie/32647/1/R-de-Visser-Dry-January-evaluation-2019.pdf) | Self-reported online surveys of participants; run with Alcohol Change UK; chart shape illustrative |
| WEEK 4 · "BLOOD PRESSURE AND SUGAR IMPROVE"; HEART callout "PRESSURE DOWN ~6%" | One month off alcohol in moderate-heavy drinkers: insulin resistance −25.9%, systolic BP −6.6%, diastolic −6.3%, weight −1.5% | Mehta et al., *BMJ Open* 2018;8:e020673, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29730627/ | Observational, not randomised; 94 abstainers vs 47 controls; "sugar" = insulin resistance (blood-sugar control) |
| MONTH 1 · "MORE ENERGY, MORE CONTROL" | Dry January participants reported more energy (2018) and feeling more in control of their drinking (80%, 2019) | University of Sussex releases above | Self-reported |

## Deliberately not claimed
- Liver fat or liver repair percentages (popular figures trace to non-peer-reviewed experiments).
- Skin and weight-loss claims (survey only; not shown).
- Any claim that one month undoes long-term damage.
