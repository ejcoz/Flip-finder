# 52 Cards — treatment

Style: Pixel Countdown Science. 1080×1920, 30 fps, 38 s, English, no voice. Built on the feel rules (STYLE.md §5) and the sonification model (§7).

## Idea
A deck of 52 cards can be ordered in 52! ways: an 8 followed by 67 zeros. Deal the cards one by one and the count of possible orders multiplies with each card, racing past everyone on Earth, every grain of sand, all the stars and the atoms on Earth. Punchline: a well-shuffled deck is almost certainly in an order that has never existed before.

## Facts and sources
- Orders of a 52-card deck: 52! ≈ 8.07×10⁶⁷ (68 digits). After k cards the count is 52 × 51 × … × (53 − k).
- 6 cards: 1.47×10¹⁰ > world population (~8×10⁹). 12 cards: 9.9×10¹⁹ > grains of sand on Earth (~7.5×10¹⁸, a common estimate). 15 cards: 5.9×10²⁴ > stars in the observable universe (~10²⁴, upper estimates). 17 cards: 7.8×10²⁷ > atoms in a human body (~7×10²⁷). 33 cards: 6.6×10⁵⁰ > atoms on Earth (~1.3×10⁵⁰).
- "All of us since the Big Bang": 8×10⁹ people × 4.35×10¹⁷ s (13.8 billion years) = 3.5×10²⁷ shuffles, about 4×10⁻⁴¹ of all orders (4×10⁻³⁹ %).
- "A world first" holds for a well-mixed deck (about seven riffle shuffles: Bayer & Diaconis, 1992). It is a probability statement, not a certainty: the chance of repeating any earlier order is vanishingly small. A brand-new deck out of the box starts in a standard order, so it must actually be shuffled.
- The grains-of-sand and star counts are order-of-magnitude estimates; the comparisons hold with wide margins.
