# How Rare Are You? — treatment

Style: Pixel Countdown Science. 1080×1920, 30 fps, 43.5 s, English, no voice. Built on the feel rules (STYLE.md §5) and the sonification model (§7).

## Why it is built for the masses
The earlier films were interesting but impersonal. This one is about the viewer's own body, and it is designed to give a reason for each action:

| Action | The reason this film gives |
|---|---|
| Comment | Everyone ends with a personal score out of 5 to report, and "which ones can you do?" |
| Share | It is a challenge to try with or against a friend ("send it to a friend") |
| Like | It flatters: most people find at least one thing they can do that most can't |
| Follow | It is plainly part 1 of a series ("follow for part 2") |

## Structure: five rounds, escalating from common to rare
The film runs on a 120 bpm grid. Each round is 3 bars (6 s): read the test (1 s) → try it during a 3-2-1 countdown, one count per second, while the hero strains at it too → on the next downbeat the hero nails it and a crowd of 100 lights up to show the share of people who can (2 s).

| t (s) | Caption | Share shown |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | HOW RARE ARE YOU? / 5 TESTS. KEEP SCORE | — |
| 4–10 | 1. ROLL YOUR TONGUE → ~7 IN 10 | ~70 % |
| 10–16 | 2. BEND YOUR THUMB BACKWARDS → ~3 IN 10 | ~30 % |
| 16–22 | 3. WIGGLE YOUR EARS → ~2 IN 10 | ~20 % |
| 22–28 | 4. MAKE A CLOVER WITH YOUR TONGUE → ~1 IN 7 | ~15 % |
| 28–34 | 5. TOUCH TONGUE TO YOUR NOSE → ~1 IN 10 | ~10 % |
| 34–34.5 | true silence | — |
| 34.5–38.5 | ALL FIVE? ROUGHLY / 1 IN 1,600 PEOPLE (the hero becomes one dot among 1,600) | — |
| 38.5–43.5 | COMMENT YOUR SCORE / FOLLOW FOR PART 2 / SEND IT TO A FRIEND | — |

Sound: two layers. A backing track (soft kick, chip bass, pulsing chords) runs under the whole film, home for the instruction, a tense chord under the countdown, home again on the reveal, and a semitone higher each round. On top, the event hits: The countdown is three rising hits; the reveal is a chord hit; then the crowd lights up as a run of notes, about one per three people, so a common ability is a long rushing run and a rare one is only a few notes.

## Facts and sources (all approximate; older and smaller studies, varying by population)
- **Tongue rolling (tube):** 65–81 % of people can (Sturtevant 1940; Komai 1951; reviewed by McDonald, *Myths of Human Genetics*, 2011). Shown as ~70 %.
- **Hitchhiker's thumb** (bending the top joint of the thumb back by 50° or more): about 24–36 % (Glass & Kistler 1953; McDonald 2011). Shown as ~30 %.
- **Ear wiggling:** commonly cited at 10–20 % of people. Shown as ~20 %; this is the softest number here.
- **Cloverleaf tongue:** about 15 % in a US sample (Whitney 1950, 14.7 %). Shown as ~15 % ("~1 in 7").
- **Tongue to nose (Gorlin sign):** about 10 % of the general population (Gorlin; far more common in Ehlers–Danlos syndrome). Shown as ~10 %.
- **"All five: roughly 1 in 1,600"** = 0.70 × 0.30 × 0.20 × 0.15 × 0.10 ≈ 0.00063, assuming the five are independent. They are not fully independent (the two tongue traits, and joint flexibility, are related), so the real figure is likely less rare. The film says "roughly".
- None of these is harmful to try, but "bend your thumb backwards" means letting the top joint bend back on its own, not forcing it.
