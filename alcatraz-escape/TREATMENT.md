# Alcatraz: Case Open — treatment

Halftone Dossier, vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps, English. The user's own narration file (`voice/narration.mp3`) is used as it is, with no TTS. The film is timed to it: the voice starts at 1.00 s and the last word ("case") ends at 33.53 s, so the film is **33.65 s**. The brief asked for 35 s, but the voice is 32.8 s long and the film has to end on the last spoken word, so the voice sets the length.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The declassified file read in order.** Each narration sentence is an exhibit card (01–07) in the case folder. The camera moves from exhibit to exhibit, and the folder closes at the end. *Against:* it's tidy, but it opens on a folder rather than the island, and the brief wants the island first.
2. **Searchlight as the thread (chosen).** A searchlight sweep opens the film on the island. Every scene then has one moving light source: a lamp on the cell doors, a drill bit's sparks, a guard's flashlight over the bed, the searchlight again missing the rooftop, and finally morning light. The light keeps missing the men until the head is found, and the file stays open. The halftone dots are the light: dot density follows each beam. This makes "being looked for and not found" the visual idea, which is the film's subject.
3. **Countdown to the bed check.** A clock chip counts from lights-out to the morning count, and each scene is a time stamp. *Against:* the narration isn't chronological by the clock, so the chip would argue with the voice.

Choice: **2**. It gives one native move with a reason (the dot field *is* the beam), a bookend (the search beam that opens the film and the searchlight that never finds them), and a strong payoff in scene 4, where the beam slides over the head without stopping.

## 2. Logline and arc

Three men leave Alcatraz under the searchlight's nose, leaving dummies in their beds, and the case is still open.
- Setup: the island at night, the hook "They were never found". The three cells, the drill.
- Turn: the fourth man is stuck. The dummy head takes the guard's flashlight and wins.
- Ending: a raft into the dark, a head rolling off a pillow in the morning, a wanted poster stamped CASE OPEN, ending on "case."

## 3. Benchmark

- **Saul Bass–era crime title sequences** (cut paper, hard shapes, silhouettes) and **1960s pulp true-crime magazine covers** (Ben-Day dots, two or three spot inks, screaming slab headlines). Learn: silhouettes as the only figures, light as a shape, one bold headline per frame. Don't take: their layouts, lettering or any specific images.
- **Vertical true-crime explainers** for hook placement: the claim goes up front in the first frame, and the captions carry the voice. Learn the pacing, not their look.

## 4. Look

Paper: warm cream, multiplied over everything. Inks: **navy** (dark ink, outlines, night ground), **cobalt** (overprint shadow, water, night dots), **yellow** (highlight: keywords, searchlight, raincoats), **red** (stamp, name-tag tape, alarm only). Local colours on the dummy head only: flesh and brown hair. Night scenes use a navy ground with lighter cobalt and yellow dots, still multiplied by the paper. People are solid navy silhouettes (from behind or in profile, with no faces drawn). Hands in scene 3 are flat silhouettes.

Type: hook and exhibit headlines in **Alfa Slab One** with a cobalt +8/+8 misregistration shadow. Captions in **Archivo Black** (bold sans) in the dossier caption pill, 2–4 words per card, centred at y = 1248 (65 %). Name tags in **Bagel Fat One**. HUD in **JetBrains Mono 800**. No text, HUD or caption below y = 1536 (bottom 20 %).
