Every on-screen claim, its source and the wording it rests on. Retrieved 2026-11-18.

|---|---|---|---|
| HOUR 2; caption "HALF YOUR / NICOTINE IS GONE"; readout 100→50% | Plasma half-life of nicotine is about 2 hours | Jones & Benowitz, ACNP *Neuropsychopharmacology* chapter (https://acnp.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/C107_1533-1544.pdf): "nicotine's 2-hour half-life"; Yale teaching notes citing Benowitz, *Clin Pharmacol Ther* 2008 (https://drillkit.yale.edu/concepts/substance-abuse-dependence/tobacco/lesson-817/objective-821): "the ½ life of nicotine in plasma is about 2 hours"; review: Benowitz, Hukkanen & Jacob (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2953858) | Plasma nicotine. Its metabolite cotinine lasts longer (~16 h half-life) |
| Readout 0% by DAYS 1-3 | After ~36 half-lives (72 h) plasma nicotine is effectively zero | arithmetic from the 2-hour half-life above | "0%" = rounded; cotinine can still be detected |
| HEART callout "NICOTINE / WEARING OFF" | Nicotine is a stimulant that raises heart rate; the effect fades as levels fall | Benowitz review above (cardiovascular effects of nicotine) | Deliberately no heart-rate numbers |
| DAYS 1-3; caption "WITHDRAWAL PEAKS: / CRAVINGS, BAD SLEEP"; BRAIN callout "CRAVES / NICOTINE" | Withdrawal is worst in the first week, peaking in the first 3 days; symptoms include cravings, irritability, trouble sleeping | National Cancer Institute, *Handling Nicotine Withdrawal and Triggers* (https://www.cancer.gov/cancertopics/factsheet/Tobacco/symptoms-triggers-quitting): "usually worst during the first week after quitting, peaking during the first 3 days"; NHS (https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/quit-smoking/staying-smoke-free/managing-nicotine-withdrawal-symptoms) | Sources are about nicotine withdrawal generally (mostly smoking) |
| WHY SO HARD?; caption "YOUR BRAIN GREW / EXTRA RECEPTORS" | Nicotine use raises the number of nicotinic receptors in the brain (upregulation) | NIDA Notes, *Abstinent smokers' nicotinic receptors take more than a month to normalize* (https://archives.drugabuse.gov/news-events/nida-notes/2009/10/abstinent-smokers-nicotinic-receptors-take-more-than-a-month-to-normalize); Staley/Cosgrove SPECT study (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2796827) | Studied in smokers |

## Deliberately not claimed
- **Lungs.** I found no solid prospective evidence on respiratory recovery after quitting vaping specifically (studies cover smokers switching, or symptoms among current/former vapers). The lungs stay unchanged in the film.
- **Heart-rate numbers, blood pressure, taste/smell timings**: those common "timeline" figures come from smoking research and marketing pages.

The meter is illustrative, not a measurement |
